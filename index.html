Plan the request

Recording the condition of the work area before anything starts

Before work starts, the area around a heater, drain, pipe route, fixture, pit or treatment unit may already carry scuffs, stains, cracks or loose items that nobody wrote down. For a Wasaga Beach home, list what sits within reach of the work, photograph floors, walls, ceilings and cabinets with the date visible, and note existing marks in writing. If a provider later asks about a surface, you then have a plain reference rather than a recollection.

  • Dated photographs

    Photograph the floor, walls and cabinets nearest the work, wide and close up, with the date recorded by your device.

  • Existing marks

    Write down each scratch, stain, chip or crack you can already see, and which photograph shows it.

  • Items moved aside

    List what you cleared from the area, such as stored boxes or cabinet contents, and where each item was placed.

Before you send

What to Photograph Before Requesting a Quote

Clear photos help a provider understand the job before anyone visits. Keep them handy for the follow-up call or email.

  • Water heater: the label with the model and capacity, the vent connection at the top of the tank, and any rental sticker or agreement.
  • Blocked drain: the fixtures that drain slowly or back up, whether it's only the lowest level, and the floor drain or cleanout if it's accessible.
  • Leak: the stain or damp patch, the water meter dial with every tap closed, and any recent renovation nearby.
  • Sump pump: the open pit and the pump inside it, the discharge pipe where it exits, and any backup pump or alarm.
  • Older supply pipe: the pipe beside the water meter. Copper looks orange-brown, galvanized steel is grey and threaded, and lead is dull grey and turns shiny silver where you scratch it with a key.
FAQ

Questions About Your Wasaga Beach Request

If I send dated photos with my Wasaga Beach request, has a visit been booked?

No. A request only starts a review: our enquiry team reads it, and any provider must separately confirm the address, the type of work, their qualifications, the written scope, pricing and scheduling. Coverage, availability and price are not confirmed by sending. Until all of that is agreed, nothing has been booked and nothing is owed.

How should I photograph the area around work in my Wasaga Beach home?

Take one wide photograph from the doorway or stairs, then close-up shots of each floor, wall, cabinet and ceiling section within reach of the work. Keep the date visible on the device, hold the camera at a steady height, and add a short written note of any mark you can already see. Do not open or move anything that is fixed in place.

Should photographs be sent with a Wasaga Beach plumbing request?

Yes, where you can. Photographs of the work area, the equipment label and any existing marks let the respondent see the starting condition and ask better questions. If you have none yet, describe the area in writing and say what has been moved, and our enquiry team will still review the request.

Can one request cover a drain and a fixture in my Wasaga Beach house?

Yes, if the items are connected or share a room; list each item and room separately and photograph each location. Unrelated items, such as a softener question and a bathroom tap, are usually clearer as two requests, so the written scope for each can be agreed on its own.

What should a written record say if something is marked during plumbing work?

It should list the agreed work and, as a separate item, any mark, chip, stain or loose finish noticed during it, with where it was and when. Compare it with your dated photographs. A new mark is a question to settle with the provider in writing, and a record is only a reference, not a finding of fault.

Who decides what has to be moved before work begins?

The respondent says what access is needed, and you decide whether to agree to it. Ask what must be cleared, what will be covered and who will do the moving, and note the answers. If an item is fragile or fixed in place, say so in your request. A condominium corporation or landlord may need to approve access, where one applies.
