Questions About the Work

Which chips or stains around the toilet or vanity already exist?

Replacing a toilet or vanity can reveal a ring, a cracked tile or a worn cabinet floor, and a dated photograph helps separate what was there already.

  • Photograph the fixture, its shutoff and the floor

    Ask the respondent to confirm which fixtures are affected, then photograph each one with its stop valve, supply line and drain connection visible. Add a wide view of the floor, the tile edge and any caulk line, and a close photograph of the model name where one is printed. Do not force a stop valve that will not turn.

  • Compare replacing a fixture with resetting it

    Compare replacing a fixture with resetting the existing one, since a new base or footprint may cover or expose different floor and wall surfaces. Ask the respondent to say what will be removed, and note any existing gap or crack beside your photographs first.

  • Get a written list of what changed in the room

    Have the provider list in writing which fixture parts were replaced, which were kept and what was found loose, cracked or discoloured beneath them. Photograph the same floor, tile and wall in your Wasaga Beach home again once the work is finished and keep both sets together.
